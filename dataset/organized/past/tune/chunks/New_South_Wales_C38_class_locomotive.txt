New_South_Wales_C38_class_locomotive | History
 The 38 class briefly returned to the former Melbourne Limited Express route in April 1962 , when 3830 and 3813 hauled the inaugural standard gauge Spirit of Progress from Albury to Sydney . The first 38 class locomotive was withdrawn in 1961 with the last withdrawn in December 1970 . In August 1970 , 3801 pulled the Western Endeavour to Perth after the Sydney–Perth rail corridor was converted to standard gauge with 3813 assisting as far as Port Pirie . In April 1988 , 3801 again operated to Perth during the Australian Bicentenary .

=== CHUNK /wiki/New_South_Wales_C38_class_locomotive#5@past ===
New_South_Wales_C38_class_locomotive | History
In May 1939 , an order for five 38 class locomotives was placed with English Electric . They suffered many delays during construction , mostly due to resource shortages caused by World War II and the Great Depression . The first five locomotives , built by Clyde Engineering , had a semi-streamlined boiler casing . The design , however , as with many others , did possess a teething trouble : no fireman could maintain steam in the 245 psi boiler – the highest boiler pressure of any engine in Australia .
