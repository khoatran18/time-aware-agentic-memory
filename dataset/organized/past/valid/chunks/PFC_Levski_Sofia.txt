PFC_Levski_Sofia | History
After World War II , Levski became one of the two top clubs in Bulgaria . After winning the championship in 1946 , 1947 , 1949 , 1950 and 1953 , Levski would not capture the domestic title again until the mid-1960s . In 1951 , the authorities changed the clubs name to Dinamo following the Soviet traditions , but after the de-Stalinization of Bulgaria , it was reverted in 1957 . The 1960s were marked with return to success both on the domestic and on the international stage . Levskis academy would become the most successful in national youth

The championship title of the club for 1985 was suspended . However , the suspensions were lifted shortly after . Levski captured another cup and league titles in 1986 and 1988 , respectively . The fourth European quarter-final came in 1986–87 , when Levski knocked out the 1985–86 Danish Cup winners Boldklubben 1903 and the 1985–86 Yugoslav Cup holders Velež Mostar , before losing to the 1985–86 Copa del Rey winners Real Zaragoza .
